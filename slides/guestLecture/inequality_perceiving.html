A Perspective of Economic Inequality

Advancing Global Mental Health: Cultural Commonalities and Differences

The potential cognitive risk

  • The world is more unequal than at any point since World War II
  • Yet mass demand for redistribution has not risen to match (Why?)

Economic inequality: The uneven distribution of wealth (e.g., income) among individuals or groups within a society.

  • What environment are we actually living in?
  • How does living in an unequal society shape citizens’ minds?
  • What if exposure to inequality becomes unavoidable?

Roadmap for the talk: three linked questions, two countries (US + China), one claim: inequality changes minds toward indifference and even conservatism, not toward demand for redistribution.

What environment?

Objective inequality vs. perceived inequality

Are we living in an (economically) unequal world?

How do you know?

Common measures

  • S80/S20 Ratio: The richest 20% / the poorest 20%
  • Palma Ratio: The richest 10% / the poorest 40%
  • Gini Coefficient: The cumulative percentage of total income / the cumulative percentage of the population, starting from the poorest

Each measure looks at a different slice of the distribution, which is exactly why they can disagree about the same society.

Illustration

The measures agree on the ranking here because the simulation is clean. Real distributions rarely are.

Ready for the real world?

  • 0.4 Gini: the richest 40% control about 60~70% of the fortune
  • 0.6 Gini: the richest 20~30% control about 70~80%
  • China’s Gini has stayed above 0.4 since 2000, through SOE layoffs, the financial crisis, and COVID-19.

What people see is not what the data say

Objective and perceived inequality move apart, and it is the perceived one that travels with political trust. This is the first hint that the story is cognitive, not merely economic.

Why don’t people ask for more?

What we have known: inequality is bad for minds and for the social fabric (Cozzolino 2011; Payne et al. 2017; Solt 2008)

  • Why don’t citizens in unequal societies demand more redistribution? (Belguise et al. 2025; Hoy and Mager 2021; Whyte 2010)
  • Tunnel effect: seeing the next lane move gives you hope rather than anger, because you expect to move soon yourself.
  • The social-volcano thesis (He 2003) predicts the opposite: inequality ↑ → unrest ↑ → stability ↓. The volcano has stayed dormant.

My answer

Both standard answers appear limited when:

  • the economy develops slower, so the wall is no longer hidden, and
  • people come to know, so exposure is no longer optional.

The economic environment changes minds → mass indifference, even conservatism, in redistribution preferences.

This is the thesis of the talk. The rest of the slides build it in two steps and one test:

  1. How: the environment reshapes class consciousness and meritocratic belief (US).
  2. When/why: it travels through perceptions of the closest institution (China).
  3. What if: forcing people to compare still does not produce uniform demand for redistribution.

How

How does the economic environment reshape your beliefs about your own society?

Economic inequality → social role

  • Conflict theory: under high inequality, the poor have higher class consciousness and believe in meritocracy less.
  • Relative power theory: under high inequality, the poor do not gain class consciousness, and may believe in meritocracy more.
  • Class: bourgeoisie /bou-zhua-zi/, proletariat
  • Meritocracy is the key to the American Dream and the hope of the poor
    • Self-reinforcing: relative power theory
    • Self-negating: conflict theory
  • The stake is how a society reproduces itself.

Take-away

Higher inequality → less class-conscious and more attached to meritocracy

Self-negating vs. self-reproducing. Relative power theory gets the support:

  1. Economic inequality reduces people’s class consciousness
  2. Economic inequality raises the poor’s belief in meritocracy

Have-nots (Newman et al. 2015): “Some people think of American society as divided into two groups, the ‘haves’ and the ‘have-nots,’ while others think it’s incorrect to think of America that way. When (why)

Through what channels does the macro environment reach the micro mind?

From “what is” to “what should be”

Perceiving ahead of judging: if inequality reshapes minds, it likely does so through evaluation, not bare awareness (Krieckhaus et al. 2014)

  • 贵港：广西壮族自治区
  • 扬州/南京：江苏
  • 抚州：江西省

People living under nearly identical Gini levels report very different perceptions.

People can feel inequality in various ways (Jin et al. 2024)

Impartial governance:

  • The damage of economic disparities on political equality in democracies has been well documented in the literature (Schlozman et al. 2013).
  • Formal and informal institutions exist in non-democracies to convert economic resources into political influence and favorable treatment.
  • Wealthy Chinese private entrepreneurs form extensive connections with local government officials to gain privileges (Chen and Dickson 2010).

Government Non-responsiveness:

  • Substantial evidence indicates that economic inequality reduces social capital and generates distrust and social conflicts.
  • Class conflict theory suggests that in highly unequal societies, people’s perceptions of economic positions on the social ladder are accentuated (Kraus et al. 2013).
  • Low levels of social capital and accentuated social conflicts make governance difficult as Baldwin and Huber (2010) showed that income differences make an agreement over which public goods to provide more difficult, therefore have a negative relationship with public goods provision (Epp and Jennings 2020).

Judicial Injustice:

  1. greater inequality implies that the well-off have more resources to control the legal apparatus while the poor are more disadvantaged.
  2. the advantaged are motivated to attempt such control because of intensified social conflicts.

The Chinese classic case: 《葫芦僧判断葫芦案》, where the magistrate follows the “protective charm” list of the four great families instead of the law.

Anti-Corruption Inadequacy:

When inequality leads to widespread corruption, citizens doubt governments’ intentions and abilities to combat corruption and perceive governments as incompetent to address the problems of the system.

What if

What happens when the wall comes down, when people are forced to compare?

Comparison → Perception

There exists, in the human organism, a drive to evaluate his opinions and abilities. The person will do this by comparing himself with others. (Festinger 1954, 117)

  • Upward comparison (vs. the rich) → discontent (Crosby 1976; Gurr 2015)
  • Downward comparison (vs. the poor) → self-esteem (Wills 1981; Hopkins and Bilimoria 2008; Gibbons and Gerrard 1989)

Comparison Effects

\(H_{1.1}\): Upward comparison → redistribution support ↑

\(H_{1.2}\): Downward comparison → redistribution support ↓

The four-worlds view holds that people simply do not compare across strata. Urbanization and social media make the walls thinner. What follows when comparison becomes unavoidable?

Institutional conditions

\(H_{3.1}\): Governmental and SOE employees → lower redistribution support
\(H_{3.2}\): The masses → higher redistribution support

Experimental Design (Inspired by Condon and Wichowsky 2020)

[In Chinese] Given the social ladder on the left, please imagine the individuals in Chinese society who are the (worst/best) off, (level 1/10), those who have the (least/most) wealth, (lowest/highest) education level, and (least respected/most respected) jobs. Specifically, consider how you differ from these individuals in terms of your own income, educational background, and job status.

Policy preferences:

Which of the following policies can help alleviate social and economic inequality and promote common prosperity? Please rank the policies below in order of importance (You don’t have to select all):

_______ Increasing the minimum living allowance (Dibao)
_______ Raising retirement pensions
_______ Raising unemployment benefits
_______ Increasing the amount of education loans

Data: China Value Survey (2021), nationally quota-representative, N = 3,603.

Participants in the control group are asked to imagine conversing with individuals who have similar income, education levels, and social status as themselves, and to reflect on what topics might arise in such a conversation.

Social comparison does “not” make a difference

No significant effects, but the estimates run in the hypothesized direction. Post-experimental adjustment: sex, age, education, urban/rural, marital status, party membership; GDP, GDP growth, population, city-level Gini; provincial fixed effects.

Two explanations

Explanation 1, self vs. others: no one is worse off than the person standing where I stand. 没有比处于自己位置的人更苦的了。

Explanation 2, insider vs. outsider:

  1. Among governmental employees, the lower-income ones report more fairness and higher expected mobility; the higher-income ones do not.
  2. SOE employees voice policy demands, but mostly for policies benefiting people like themselves.
  3. The masses do not think much further.

Take-home points

  • What environment: an economic fact, but one that people read through sociopolitical lenses
  • How it works: it reshapes beliefs about dreams and about power
  • What if the wall falls: comparison alone does not move preferences, because institutions sort who feels what

Psychological health tips

Knowing what you feel about inequality, learning why you feel so, and leading how you feel

The cognitive factors nested in institutional structures keep the “social volcano” dormant, but people are neither unaware nor indifferent.

Open questions: alternative explanations for the “what if” findings; what governments can do; the role of communication technology in this process.
