Youth Political Socialization

  • Three pathways of political socialization in Chinese universities
    • Ideological and political theory courses → the classroom setting
    • Party and League activities → organized socialization: widest coverage, highest institutionalization
    • Volunteer activities → voluntary socialization: individual free choice

What is the use of group activities enforced by discipline?

What do they change in young people, and how?

Xi Jinping: ideological and political work runs through the entire talent-cultivation system. Empirical research on this institutionalized socialization process is key to understanding the distinctive logic of Chinese higher education.

The key difference between Party and League activities and volunteer activities lies in the degree of organization and disciplinary constraint: the former rests on charters and organizational discipline, so participation is rigid; the latter presupposes active participation and leaves more room for choice.

Does Organized Socialization Work?

Supportive view (Brady et al. 1995; Metz and Youniss 2005; Putnam 1993)

  • Institutional embedding → lower socialization costs
  • Periodic rituals → value internalization
  • Social capital → emotional bonds

Skeptical view (Scott 1985; Huang 2015)

  • Coercion → psychological reactance, performative compliance
  • High-frequency mobilization → identification fatigue
  • Competing influence of family and society → unstable effects

Let political psychology in

  • Three-layer progressive structure of attitude formation: Emotion → Behavior → Value
    • Behavioral-cognitive mechanisms: self-perception + cognitive dissonance (Bem 1972; Festinger 1957)
    • Repeated participation → self-attribution → attitude reshaping

Symbolic emotion is measured by patriotism; experiential emotion by sense of gain, happiness, and security, the latter tied to the “three senses” in policy discourse since the 19th Party Congress. The four types of value identification correspond closely, in structure, to the “Four Confidences.”

The micro-mechanism that “behavior precedes attitude” offers a way to explain the divergent positive and negative evidence in a unified manner.

Hypotheses

  • H1 (whether): Party and League activities → emotional / behavioral / value identification ↑ (a) or ↓ (b)
  • H2 (what form): threshold effect (appears upon participation) vs. cumulative effect (stronger with higher frequency)
  • H3 (which is stronger): effect of voluntary volunteer activities > discipline-bound Party and League activities (Deci and Ryan 1985)
  • H4 (who benefits): students from families outside the state system gain more → compensatory socialization (Neundorf et al. 2016)

H1 comprises three sets of competing hypotheses, corresponding to the emotional, behavioral, and value dimensions. Threshold pattern: once drawn into the political arena, identification rises initially; cumulative pattern: identification depends on sustained observation, reinforcement, and repeated exposure. H3 derives from self-determination theory: autonomous participation driven by intrinsic motivation should shape people more than discipline-bound participation.

Research Design

Data

  • T University “Student Baseline Survey” (five waves, 2019–2023)
  • Department-stratified sampling, about 10% of enrolled students each year
  • Repeated cross-sections, N = 6135

Explanatory variable: participation frequency this academic year (0 / 1–4 / 5–8 / 9–12 / 12+)

  • Organized socialization: Party-organization activities, class-based Youth League activities
  • Voluntary socialization: volunteer activities

Outcome variables: 11 indicators

  • Emotion (4): sense of gain, happiness, security, patriotism
  • Behavior (3): propensity for institutionalized / non-institutionalized expression (number of channels selected), share of institutionalized channels
  • Value (4): identification with the system, policies, culture, and path

Models

  • Linear mixed models (random intercepts for province of origin and year)
  • Threshold model (five categories) + cumulative model (linear frequency)
  • All three activity types in one model → “horse race” comparison
  • Activity × parental occupation type interaction

Controls: gender, field of study, year of study, Party/League membership, student-organization membership, family income, parental occupation type, parental education, family residence, region of origin, high-school type. Emotion and value indicators are estimated with an IRT graded response model (IRT-GRM) and rescaled to the 0–1 range. Expression propensity is measured by the number of channels respondents select in a hypothetical scenario, reflecting the breadth of expression options they are willing to consider, not participation that has actually occurred; the two channel types offer different numbers of options, so absolute levels are not directly comparable.

Threshold Effect: Does Participation Work Right Away?

1–4 times vs. 0 times

  • Party-organization activities
    • Patriotism ↑, sense of gain / happiness / security
    • Institutionalized expression ↑ & non-institutionalized expression ↓
  • Class-based Youth League activities
    • All four emotions ↑
    • Institutionalized expression ↑, non-institutionalized expression
  • Volunteer activities
    • Happiness / patriotism ↑, sense of gain / security
    • Institutionalized expression ↑, non-institutionalized expression

Low-frequency participants already show an institutionalized orientation in national sentiment and in the choice of expression channels. Party-organization activities lean toward national sentiment and institutionalized expression, class-based Youth League activities involve a broader range of emotional experience, and volunteer activities are linked to some positive emotions and to institutionalized expression.

Cumulative Effect: Does More Participation Mean More Identification?

Each one-level increase in frequency

  • Party-organization activities
    • Sense of gain / patriotism ↑, happiness / security
    • Institutionalized expression ↑ & non-institutionalized expression ↓
  • Class-based Youth League activities
    • All four emotions ↑
    • Institutionalized expression ↑, non-institutionalized expression
  • Volunteer activities
    • Happiness / patriotism ↑, security ↓
    • Institutionalized expression ↑, non-institutionalized expression

The “two-way effect” of Party-organization activities is the key finding: the higher the frequency of participation, the stronger the propensity for institutionalized expression and the weaker the propensity for non-institutionalized expression. This two-way pattern holds under both the threshold and cumulative models, and neither class-based Youth League activities nor volunteer activities achieve it.

The categorical-frequency model further shows that different outcomes require different participation intensity: patriotism and both expression propensities differ already at 1–4 times; gains in sense of gain appear mainly at 9–12 times and above.

Robustness: after excluding Party members, the effects of Party-organization activities largely hold; after excluding League members, the effects of class-based Youth League activities are relatively limited (see Appendix).

Value Identification: Whose Shaping Is More Comprehensive?

  • Party-organization activities: all four types of identification ↑, with the largest coefficients
  • Class-based Youth League activities: all four types ↑, weaker than Party-organization activities
  • Volunteer activities: identification with the system / policies ↑, culture / path


  • Gradient structure: the more concentrated the political content and the deeper the organizational embedding → the broader the association with value identification
  • Disciplined ≥ voluntary

Regardless of the degree of organization, sustained socialization practice can internalize identification at the value level, but Party-organization activities show a clearer advantage over volunteer activities in comprehensiveness and consistency. This runs contrary to the expectation of self-determination theory that autonomous participation is more easily internalized.

Moderating Effect: Compensatory Integration

  • Party-organization activities × families outside the state system: marginal gains in sense of gain and policy identification are significantly larger
  • All other outcomes, and the interaction terms for class-based Youth League activities, are not significant

Students from families inside the state system already enter university with a higher starting level of political identification, so there is limited room for further gains; students from families outside the system gain more compensation from institutionalized education. Party-organization activities do not simply reinforce existing political-resource advantages; to some extent they make up for differences in family political-socialization resources.

Conclusions and Implications

  1. Organized socialization is consistently and positively associated with identification, but with different emphases: Party-organization activities → patriotism, institutionalized expression, value identification; class-based Youth League activities → campus emotional experience; volunteer activities show limited significant associations
  2. Threshold + cumulative patterns coexist: patriotism and expression-channel propensity appear at low frequency; sense of gain depends on sustained participation
  3. Disciplined ≥ voluntary → effectiveness depends on institutional depth, breadth of coverage, and sustained predictability, not on a voluntary/compulsory dichotomy
  4. Compensatory integration → students from families outside the state system gain more in sense of gain and policy identification

The dialectical unity of “strictness” and “care,” of “management” and “nurturing,” in ideological and political work. Disciplinary constraint does not necessarily produce identification directly, but it steadily brings students with low willingness to participate into the political-socialization arena, creating opportunities for them to repeatedly encounter political content and organizational norms.

Interviews with 51 students at 17 universities corroborate the functional differentiation: participants in class-based Youth League activities mostly mention everyday interaction and organizational belonging (“finding an organization”), while participants in Party-organization activities more often mention theoretical study and ritual experience (understanding “faith” after visiting a martyrs’ cemetery).

Limitations: a single university and repeated cross-sections, so reverse selection cannot be fully ruled out.

Appendix

Robustness: Excluding Organization Members

  • Excluding Party members: Party-organization activities → patriotism, institutionalized expression, and all four types of identification ↑; non-institutionalized expression ↓
  • Excluding League members: class-based Youth League activities → patriotism and policy identification ↑; happiness, cultural identification, and institutionalized expression significant only at the 10% level

Categorical-Frequency Model: Required Participation Intensity

Outcome First significant frequency group Pattern
Patriotism 1–4 times Threshold + cumulative
Propensity for institutionalized expression 1–4 times Threshold + cumulative
Propensity for non-institutionalized expression ↓ 1–4 times Threshold + cumulative
Sense of gain 9–12 times and above Cumulative
  • Party-organization activities, 0-times group: Party members 0.9%, League members 84.3% → participation is related to, but does not coincide with, membership status
  • Models control for both Party and League membership

Interview Sample and Implementation Notes

  • August–December 2024: 51 enrolled students at 17 universities nationwide
  • 12 each from 985 / 211 universities; 9 each from Tier-1 / Tier-2 undergraduate institutions and junior colleges
  • 26 men / 25 women; 25 Party members / 26 non-members
  • Semi-structured interviews: type and frequency of participation, emotional experience and attitude change, understanding of channels, motivation and incentives
  • Used only to help explain differences between activity types, not for causal identification or population inference
